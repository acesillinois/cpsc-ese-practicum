## SPATIAL PREDICTION OF ECONOMICALLY OPTIMAL NITROGEN RATES FOR ILLINOIS CORN

### Priority Area(s): Corn nitrogen management, spatial analytics

#### Overview

<p>
  Predicting the economically optimal nitrogen rate (EONR) for corn (Zea mays L.) remains challenging
because crop response to nitrogen (N) varies across locations, years, soils, and weather conditions
(Morris et al., 2018). Illinois currently uses the Maximum Return to Nitrogen (MRTN) approach, which
represents an important improvement over previous recommendation systems. However, MRTN
recommendations are provided for three broad regions—northern, central, and southern Illinois—and may
not adequately capture local variability, particularly near regional boundaries. Previous research has
demonstrated the potential of incorporating soil properties into locally based EONR prediction models
(Qin et al., 2018). Building on the existing MRTN approach, incorporating trial coordinates, soil properties,
and weather information into a spatial prediction framework could provide more locally tailored EONR
estimates and improve N management decisions.
</p>

#### Goals and Objectives

<p>
  The overall goal of this project is to develop a user-friendly decision-support tool that allows Illinois corn
growers to enter their field coordinates and receive a locally predicted EONR rather than relying solely on
broad regional recommendations. 
</p>
<p>
The specific objectives are to: 
  <list>
    (1) estimate site-year EONRs from historical corn N-response trials; 
    (2) develop a spatial model incorporating geographic location, soil properties, and weather conditions; and 
    (3) evaluate its performance against the current three-region MRTN approach and 
    (4) implement the best-performing model in a prototype tool.
    </list>
</p>
