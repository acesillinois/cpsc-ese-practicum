## WHERE AND WHY CORN FAILS: EIGHTEEN YEARS OF CLIMATE DRIVEN CROP INSURANCE LOSS RATIO ANALYSIS ACROSS CORN BELT
### Overview
<p>
  Drought and excess moisture are the leading cause of corn yield loss in the United
States. The federal crop insurance program is one of the instruments through which
producers absorb those losses. Every insurance claim is an adjudicated record of what
destroyed a crop and what it cost. The recorded claims is the revealed map of climate
fragility and a ledger of where public risk transfer dollars go. Yet, nearly all research on
these losses stops at the county level, coarser than the breeding, drainage and rating
decisions it should inform. This eighteen years (2008-2025) of USDA Risk Management
Agency (RMA) Cause-of-Loss (CoL) records for counties in thirteen Corn Belt states,
together with climate, soil, and farm structure data is used as a signal of climate fragility.
Losses cluster strongly every year, but the map migrates with the weather. Drought
dominates the west and excess moisture the east. Local models fit three times better
than global models, and similarity model trained only on claims redicovers the region’s
climate structure and identifies high loss counties. Therefore, the central hypothesis is
the CoL contains spatially structured climate signal. County loss ratios reflect a latent
property of the land, and its climate fragility. 
</p>

### Objectives
<list>
  
  * <b> Objective 1:</b> Identify spatial and temporal patterns of crop insurance loss risk to
fins where high loss ratios occur, whether they cluster and whether they persist
over time
  
  * <b> Objective 2:</b> Explain high loss ratios using climate, soil and land structure and
yield conditions to answer the question ‘are high loss ratios really climate driven
(episoidical) or are they linked to land quality (structural) production conditions
  
  *  <b> Objective 3:</b> Develop AI based models that incorporate spatial vulnerability
indicatiosr as prediction tools to answer the question ‘where and why corn fails’
  
</list>
