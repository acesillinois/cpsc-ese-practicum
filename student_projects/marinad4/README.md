#CORN ECONOMIC OPTIMUM NITROGEN RATES ACROSS FOUR DECADES AFFECTED BY CROP ROTATION
###Priority Area(s): Nutrient management, crop rotation, nitrogen use efficiency
####Overview
<p>
Food security can be maintained with high-yielding crops. High-yielding corn depends on nitrogen (N)
inputs. N fertilizer has a cost, and efficient use is desired for both environmental and economic reasons.
Corn crop rotation with soybean is known to reduce the need for N by about 40 kg-N ha-1. The economic
optimum N rate (EONR) is the value that maximizes partial profit, considering the price of N in relation to
the value of grain. Although EONR is known to vary by year and across fields given the differences in
weather and variability in soils and crop condition across regions, little is known about its long-term
behavior. Studies have evaluated trends for EONR regionally in the Midwest, but it remains difficult to
distinguish a temporal change in a consistent production system. Evaluating how EONR changed on a
large year scale can reveal whether the benefits of crop rotation remain consistent over time and how we
can better support future N recommendations.
  </p>
####Goals and Objectives
<p>
The goal of this project is to determine how corn yield response to N and EONR has changed over 43
years under corn-corn and corn-soybean rotations. The specific objectives are: 1) Estimate annual corn
yield response functions and EONR for each rotation 2) Explore weather data and its relation to annual
yield responses 3) Quantify long term trends in EONR 4) Interpret what is the magnitude of the trends
and what are the differences between rotations.
</p>
